📋 Types of Environmental Pollution
💨
Air Pollution

Contamination by harmful gases, particulates and chemicals. Greenhouse gases, Smog, Acid Rain, Ozone depletion.

💧
Water Pollution

Contamination by industrial, agricultural and domestic waste. BOD, Eutrophication, heavy metals.

🪨
Soil Pollution

Contamination by pesticides, heavy metals, industrial waste. DDT is a persistent soil pollutant.

🔊
Sound Pollution

Noise can damage hearing; NIOSH’s occupational reference is 85 dBA averaged over 8 hours, not a universal instant-harm threshold

☢️
Radiation / Radioactive Pollution

Pollution caused by radioactive substances and nuclear waste.

⚠️ Exam Trap
  • DDT = Dichloro Diphenyl Trichloroethane — persistent pollutant (stays in environment)
  • PCBs = Polychlorinated Biphenyls = carcinogenic industrial pollutant
🌡️ Global Warming & Greenhouse Effect
📘 Greenhouse Effect

Greenhouse gases trap heat in Earth's atmosphere → increases Earth's temperature → Global Warming.

GREENHOUSE GASES:

CO₂
CH₄ (Methane)
N₂O
CFC (Freon)
Water vapour
⚠️ Exam Trap — Greenhouse Gases
  • CO₂ is the largest contributor among long-lived anthropogenic greenhouse gases; distinguish this from the natural greenhouse effect
  • Methane’s 100-year GWP is about 27–30× CO₂ in the OP reference; it is not the most potent greenhouse gas known
  • CFC is both greenhouse gas AND ozone depleter
🌫️ Smog
General Smog
Reducing mixture
  • Composition: Smoke + Fog + SO₂
  • Nature: Reducing
  • Common in industrial areas with coal burning
Photochemical Smog
Oxidative nature
  • Formed from: Oxides of Nitrogen + Hydrocarbons (in sunlight)
  • Nature: Oxidative
  • Important secondary components: O₃, PAN, NO₂ and aldehydes
📘 PAN (Peroxy Acetyl Nitrate)

Key component of photochemical smog. Causes eye irritation and plant damage. Formula: CH₃CO·O₂NO₂

⚠️ Exam Trap — Smog
  • PAN = peroxyacetyl nitrate; it belongs to the broader peroxyacyl-nitrate family
  • Photochemical smog = Oxidative; General smog = Reducing
  • Photochemical-smog components: O₃, PAN, NO₂ and aldehydes
🌧️ Acid Rain
📘 Acid Rain

Rainwater with pH below 5.6. Normal rain has pH 5.6 (slightly acidic due to dissolved CO₂).

Normal Rain pH
5.6
Acid Rain pH
< 5.6
Responsible Gases
SO₂ + NO₂
  • Responsible gases: SO₂ and NO₂ (from industrial emissions, vehicles)
  • Damages historic monuments (marble/limestone buildings)
  • Harms soil, water bodies, forests
  • Marble cancer = acid rain eroding Taj Mahal
⚠️ Exam Trap — Acid Rain
  • Acid rain pH = below 5.6 (NOT below 7)
  • Caused by SO₂ and NO₂ (NOT CO₂)
  • CO₂ makes normal rain slightly acidic (pH 5.6) but that is NOT acid rain
🌐 Ozone Depletion
📘 Ozone Layer

Present in the Stratosphere. Protects Earth from harmful UV radiation. CFC destroys ozone molecules.

  • Location: Stratospheric pollution
  • Main cause: CFC (Chlorofluorocarbon) and Halogens
  • Freon is a historical trade name for several halocarbon refrigerants, including some CFCs
  • CFC composition: Compounds of C, Cl and F
  • CFCs were historically used as refrigerants, propellants, foam-blowing agents and solvents; many uses have been phased out
  • CFC breakdown releases chlorine radicals that catalytically destroy many ozone molecules before being removed
⚠️ Exam Trap — Ozone
  • CFC = Chlorofluorocarbon = Freon = depletes ozone
  • CFCs: historical refrigerants/propellants; many uses phased out
  • Ozone depletion = Stratospheric pollution (NOT tropospheric)
  • Ozone hole first discovered over Antarctica
  • CFC is also a greenhouse gas (dual effect)

🌿 Eutrophication
📘 Eutrophication

Process where excess nutrients (nitrates, phosphates from fertilisers) enter water bodies → Algal bloom → Respiration and microbial decomposition of bloom biomass consume dissolved O₂ → Depletion of O₂ in water → Fish and aquatic life die.

  • Caused by excess nitrates and phosphates (from agricultural runoff, sewage)
  • → Algal bloom (rapid growth of algae)
  • → Dissolved O₂ depleted
  • → Death of aquatic life (fish kill)
⚠️ Exam Trap — Eutrophication

Excess nutrients promote algal blooms; respiration and microbial decomposition of biomass can deplete dissolved O₂ and harm aquatic life

⚠️ Exam Trap — Metal Limits
  • Of Mn, Cu and Cd, cadmium has the lowest listed WHO health-based value: 0.003 mg/L. Guideline order is not a universal toxicity ranking.
  • WHO sets no health-based guideline value for zinc at typical drinking-water levels; taste/acceptability still matters
  • Fe: no WHO health-based value; Al: operational residual targets, not a universal 0.2 mg/L health limit
  • PCBs (Polychlorinated Biphenyls) = carcinogenic industrial pollutant in water

  • MIC is used in making pesticides
♻️ Green Chemistry
📘 Green Chemistry

Design of chemical products and processes that reduce or eliminate chemical hazards to humans and the environment.

  • Water can be a safer solvent when suitable: non-flammable and generally low in toxicity. Green chemistry also considers the whole process.
  • Aim: reduce pollution at the source (prevention over cure)
  • 12 principles of Green Chemistry
Oilzapper Technology
Developed by TERI
  • TERI = The Energy and Resources Institute
  • Oilzapper uses a microbial consortium for oil spills, oily sludge and petroleum-contaminated soil
