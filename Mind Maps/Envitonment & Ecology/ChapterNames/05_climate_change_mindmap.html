  • Climate Change refers to long-term shifts in climate patterns, driven by both natural and man-made causes.
  • This chapter covers causes & mitigation, the Greenhouse Effect, Global Warming, Ozone Layer depletion, India's national missions, and the major international milestones from Stockholm (1972) through COP30, Belém (2025).

🏠 Greenhouse Effect

🔥 Global Warming
  • Long-term rise in global average surface temperature, mainly driven by human-added greenhouse gases in the modern era.
  • Impacts: melting of glaciers, rise in sea level, impact on biodiversity, impact on human health, impact on climate, impact on vegetation, etc.
  • Photosynthesis response depends on species, baseline temperature, CO2, water and nutrients; heat/drought stress can reduce it, while moderate warming can increase it in some plants.
⚠ EXAM TRAP Greenhouse Effect is a natural, necessary phenomenon (keeps Earth warm enough for life) — Global Warming is the harmful excess of this effect caused by rising GHG concentrations. Don't treat them as synonyms.
🛡 Ozone Layer
LocationStratosphere; most ozone roughly 15-35 km above the surface
Maximum concentrationWithin the ozone-layer region; peak varies with latitude and season
FunctionProtects living beings from ultraviolet rays
Measured inDobson Units

Impacts of Ozone Depletion

  • Human health issues (e.g. skin cancer)
  • Food production affected
  • Aquatic ecosystem disruption

Causes of Ozone Depletion

  • Chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs)
  • Halons
  • Carbon tetrachloride and methyl chloroform
  • Methyl bromide
  • HCFCs; controlled ODS release chlorine/bromine in the stratosphere

⚠ EXAM TRAP Ozone hole was discovered in Antarctica in 1985, but the Montreal Protocol to protect the ozone layer was signed 2 years later, in 1987. Don't confuse discovery year with protocol year.

🌐 Climate Change Related Conferences — Recent CoP Series
ConferenceYearKey Points
Katowice Conference (CoP-24)2018The Katowice Climate Package, commonly called the Paris Agreement rulebook, setting detailed implementation guidelines.
Madrid Conference (CoP-25)2019Parties strengthened work on loss and damage, including creation of the Santiago Network, but failed to reach final agreement on Paris Agreement Article 6 carbon-market rules.
Glasgow Conference (CoP-26)2021The Glasgow Climate Pact strengthened efforts to keep 1.5°C within reach, called for phase-down of unabated coal power and inefficient fossil-fuel subsidies, and completed key parts of the Paris rulebook including Article 6.
Sharm El-Sheikh Conference (CoP-27)2022Agreement to establish new funding arrangements, including a fund, to address loss and damage for particularly vulnerable developing countries.
Dubai Conference (CoP-28)2023The first Global Stocktake called for transitioning away from fossil fuels in energy systems, tripling renewable-energy capacity and doubling the rate of energy-efficiency improvement by 2030; the Loss and Damage Fund was also operationalized.
Baku Conference (CoP-29)2024COP29 agreed a new climate-finance goal of at least USD 300 billion per year for developing countries by 2035, with efforts to scale total finance to USD 1.3 trillion per year, and finalized key Article 6 carbon-market rules; COP30 followed in Belém, Brazil, in 2025.

📜 Major International Conferences on Climate Change — Complete List
ConferenceYearKey Points
Stockholm Conference on Human Environment1972It led to the creation of the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) later in 1972.
Vienna Conference1985A framework for international cooperation to protect the ozone layer, paving the way for the Montreal Protocol.
Montreal Conference1987The Montreal Protocol on Substances that Deplete the Ozone Layer, adopted on 16 September 1987; the UN later proclaimed 16 September as Ozone Day in 1994.
Rio Conference (Earth Summit – I)1992Approved Agenda-21; birth of UNFCCC
Kyoto Conference (Kyoto Protocol)1997Legally binding emission targets for industrialized/Annex B Parties; first-period commitments averaged at least 5% below 1990 levels over 2008–2012.
Johannesburg Conference (Rio+10)2002The Johannesburg Declaration and Plan of Implementation, renewing commitments to sustainable development and implementation of Agenda 21.
Montreal Conference2005It hosted the first Meeting of the Parties to the Kyoto Protocol after its entry into force, operationalized key Kyoto rules/mechanisms and advanced discussions on post-2012 commitments.
Bali Conference2007The Bali Road Map, including the Bali Action Plan, launched negotiations on a broader post-2012 climate agreement.
Copenhagen Conference2009It recognized the scientific view that warming should be kept below 2°C and included political commitments on mitigation, transparency and climate finance, though the COP only 'took note' of the Accord.
Cancun Conference2010The Green Climate Fund, along with other institutional arrangements including the Cancun Adaptation Framework and Technology Mechanism.
Durban Conference (CoP-17)2011The Durban Platform for Enhanced Action, launching negotiations toward a new agreement applicable to all Parties, which eventually became the Paris Agreement; the conference also advanced the Green Climate Fund.
Doha Conference (CoP-18)2012The Doha Amendment established a second Kyoto commitment period for 2013–2020; participating Parties took commitments amounting to at least 18% below 1990 levels in aggregate.
Warsaw Conference (CoP-19)2013Establishment of the Warsaw International Mechanism for Loss and Damage associated with climate-change impacts.
Lima Conference (CoP-20)2014The Lima Call for Climate Action, which advanced elements for the 2015 agreement and guidance for countries' intended nationally determined contributions (INDCs).
Paris Conference (CoP-21)2015Hold warming well below 2°C above pre-industrial levels and pursue efforts to limit the increase to 1.5°C.
Marrakesh Conference (CoP-22)2016Moving from Paris Agreement adoption toward implementation, including the Marrakech Action Proclamation and stronger implementation partnerships and rule-making.
Bonn Conference (CoP-23)2017Progress on Paris implementation, launch of the Talanoa Dialogue process, adoption of the Gender Action Plan and the Koronivia Joint Work on Agriculture.
