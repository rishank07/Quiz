Jute Industry (Golden Fibre)
  • India is a leading jute-producing country and the largest producer of jute goods; Bangladesh is a major competing exporter
  • First jute mill: 1855, Rishra, Kolkata
  • Largest jute producer state: West Bengal

International Jute Organisation: established 1984, HQ Dhaka; later succeeded by International Jute Study Group.

Silk Textile Industry
Beginning of Modern Silk Manufacturing1832, Howrah (by East India Company)
World Rank2nd largest producer (1st: China)
Largest Producer in IndiaKarnataka

Four principal commercial natural-silk varieties: Mulberry, Tasar, Eri and Muga; oak tasar is a form of tasar.

Other Industries
  • Consumer-electronics manufacturing began with radio sets in the 1950s; black-and-white TV production followed in the 1960s
  • Petrochemical industry: started by private sector in 1961
  • First public sector petrochemical unit: Indian Petrochemicals Corporation Limited (IPCL), 1969 (Vadodara)
  • Country's first super phosphate factory: Ranipet (Tamil Nadu)
  • First modern woollen textile mill: 1876 (Kanpur)

Industrial Policy, 1956
Announced30th April, 1956

Industries divided into 3 categories: Government sector (17), Government + Private (12), Private.

Industrial Policy, 1970
AnnouncedFebruary, 1970

1970 Licensing Policy — Four Broad Categories

  • Core sector
  • Heavy-investment sector
  • Middle sector and unlicensed sector

Reform licensing and restrain concentration in large industrial houses; DICs were launched separately in 1978 following the 1977 policy.

Industrial Policy, 1980
Announced23rd July, 1980

Modernisation, technology upgradation, ancillarisation/nucleus plants, capacity utilisation and balanced regional development.

New Industrial Policy — LPG (1991)
Announced24th July, 1991
  • L — Liberalisation: major reduction of industrial licensing/regulation; licensing retained for selected industries
  • P — Privatisation (Nijikaran): Change in ownership/management of PSUs; direct sale of PSU companies
  • G — Globalisation: Integration of country's economy with world economy via trade and commerce
⚠ EXAM TRAP
MRTP Act (1969) was replaced by the Competition Act, 2002 — not amended, fully replaced. Both dealt with monopolistic/restrictive trade practices but under different frameworks.

New Public Sector Enterprise Policy 2021 strategic groups: Atomic Energy/Space/Defence; Transport/Telecom; Power/Petroleum/Coal/other minerals; Banking/Insurance/Financial Services.
