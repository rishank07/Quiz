What is Money?

Money: Anything that serves as a medium of exchange or payment for all goods and services.

Functions of Money

Primary Functions

  • Medium of Exchange
  • Measure / Unit of Value

Secondary Functions

  • Store of Value
  • Standard of Deferred Payments
⚠ EXAM TRAP
"Medium of Exchange" and "Measure of Value" are Primary functions; "Store of Value" and "Deferred Payments" are Secondary — a common swap in MCQs.

Liquidity of Money

Liquidity: Ability to convert assets into cash quickly without loss or delay.

Cash > Bank Demand Deposits > Bank Savings > Bank Fixed Deposits

Most liquid asset: Cash

Velocity of Money
  • Number of times a unit of money circulates in an economy within a specific period
  • Keynesian motives for holding money: transactions, precautionary and speculative

⚠ EXAM TRAP
Liquidity decreases from M₁ to M₄ (M₁ = highest liquidity, M₄ = lowest) — the reverse order is a common trap in ranking-based questions.
Fisher's Exchange Equation
MV = PT

M = Money Supply, V = Velocity of Circulation, P = Price Level, T = Transactions

⚡ QUICK RECALL
Fisher's equation of exchange: MV = PT (transactions) or MV = PY (income). Money illusion means focusing on nominal values while ignoring prices/real purchasing power; it is a distinct concept.

Inflation — Meaning
  • Continuous rise in the general price level
  • A sustained rise in the general price level reduces money's purchasing power, other things equal
  • Money illusion: focusing on nominal values while ignoring price-level changes and real purchasing power; distinct from the MV=PT accounting equation
Inflation Target in India4%, tolerance band 2%–6% (April 2026–March 2031)
Causes of Inflation
  • Demand Pull: Due to rise in demand
  • Cost Push: Due to rise in production cost

According to Keynes, actual inflation begins after the full employment point.

Types of Inflation
TypeDescription
Creeping InflationVery low, gradual inflation; no universal official cutoff
Walking InflationModerate inflation above creeping; numerical bands vary by textbook
Running InflationRapid inflation; no single universally accepted official range
Galloping InflationVery high, accelerating inflation; often double/triple-digit annual rates, but thresholds vary
Hyper InflationExtremely rapid inflation; common academic benchmark: more than 50% per month
Open InflationPrices free to rise without government intervention
Imported InflationDue to rising prices of imported raw products (e.g., petroleum)
⚠ EXAM TRAP
Creeping, walking, running and galloping are textbook labels with varying numerical bands. Hyperinflation has a commonly cited academic benchmark of over 50% per month.
Other Inflation-Related Terms
  • Disinflation: Slowdown in the rate of inflation (prices still rising, but slower)
  • Deflation: Fall in general price level (negative inflation)
  • Reflation: Deliberately raising prices by increasing money and credit
  • Stagflation: high inflation with weak/stagnant growth and high unemployment
  • Headline Inflation: Total inflation including food and energy; measured by CPI-C
  • Core Inflation: underlying inflation excluding volatile food and fuel; useful for analysis but not India's statutory inflation target
⚡ QUICK RECALL
Disinflation ≠ Deflation — Disinflation means prices are still rising but slower; Deflation means prices are actually falling.

Phillips Curve
Proposed ByA.W. Phillips
ShowsPossible short-run inverse relationship under certain conditions; no stable long-run trade-off. Phillips's original 1958 study related unemployment to money-wage inflation
Fiscal Measures (by Government)
  • Increase in direct taxes
  • Reduction in fiscal deficit — cut unnecessary expenditure, avoid deficit financing
  • Reduction in subsidies
  • Public borrowing may reduce private spending pressure; impact depends on how the government raises and spends the funds, not a one-for-one cash reduction
Monetary Measures (by RBI)
  • Increase in Repo Rate / Bank Rate — discourages borrowing
  • Increase in CRR and SLR — reduces banks' lending capacity
  • Sell government securities
  • Tight credit policy
Measures to Tackle Monetary Contraction

Fiscal

  • Increase public expenditure
  • Reduce taxes
  • Payment of loans

Monetary

  • Circulation of currency
  • Accommodative monetary policy
⚡ QUICK RECALL
To FIGHT inflation: raise rates/taxes, cut spending. To fight monetary CONTRACTION (deflationary pressure): opposite — raise spending, cut taxes, ease money supply.
