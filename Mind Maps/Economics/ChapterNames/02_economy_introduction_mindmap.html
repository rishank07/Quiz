What is Economy?

An economy is the system through which a society organizes production, distribution, exchange and consumption of goods and services.

Economic vs Non-Economic Activities
TypeDescriptionExamples
Economic ActivitiesProduction/provision of goods or services for income, livelihood or market exchangeDoctor, Teacher, Trader
Non-Economic ActivitiesPrimarily personal, social, moral or emotional motives rather than income or market exchangeUnpaid care or voluntary service without an income-earning motive
⚡ QUICK RECALL
Look at the activity and its income/livelihood or market-exchange connection; an NGO or religious institutional label alone does not make an activity non-economic.
Types of Economy (By Govt. Control)

1. Capitalist (Liberal) Economy

  • Production and price decisions made by market forces
  • Private ownership and markets dominate; government still regulates, taxes, provides public goods and addresses market failures
  • Laissez-faire = a preference for limited government interference, not literally zero government
  • Economic Objective: To earn profit
  • Private ownership; unequal distribution of resources
  • Key aspect of the free-market economy
ExamplesUSA, UK

2. Socialist (State) Economy

  • Socialist ideas predated Marx; Marx and Engels developed an influential scientific-socialist framework
  • State holds significant power; public ownership of resources
  • Economic Objective: Social welfare
  • Individual economic choice is more constrained under centralized direction
ExampleFormer Soviet Union (USSR); present-day Russia has a different, market-based mixed economy

3. Mixed Economy

  • Coexistence of both private and public sectors
  • Substantial but non-exclusive government role: regulation, public goods and possible public ownership alongside private enterprise
  • Economic Objective: Social welfare + Profit
  • Income distribution depends on institutions and policy; a mixed economy does not guarantee equality
  • Gandhian trusteeship is a separate idea of wealth held in trust for society, not the formal basis of India's mixed economy
ExampleIndian Economy
⚠ EXAM TRAP
Market-oriented systems emphasize decentralized private decisions; command systems emphasize central public control; mixed economies combine market and government roles. Socialist ideas predated Marx.

Characteristics of Indian Economy
  • Large developing/emerging mixed economy
  • Services lead the economy in output
  • Significant agricultural workforce
  • Coexistence of formal and informal sectors
  • Both private and public sectors
  • Growing industrial base; market-oriented reforms since 1991
⚡ QUICK RECALL
India is a mixed, increasingly market-oriented economy. Its model evolved through public-private coexistence, planning, regulation and post-1991 reforms; trusteeship is a separate normative idea.
Three Broad Sectors + Two Extended Conceptual Categories
5Categories: 3 official broad sectors + 2 conceptual extensions
  • Primary Sector
  • Secondary Sector
  • Tertiary Sector
  • Quaternary Sector
  • Quinary Sector
1. Primary Sector
DependencyDirectly dependent on environment
ActivitiesAgriculture, Animal Husbandry, Forestry, Fisheries, Mining
WorkersRed collar: informal label, not an official sector definition
Nominal GVA share (FY 2025–26 PE)~19.9%
2. Secondary Sector
FunctionUses primary sector output as raw material; transforms into finished goods
ActivitiesConstruction, Manufacturing (textile, bread), Electricity, Gas, Water Supply
Also CalledIndustrial Sector
WorkersBlue collar: informal occupational label, not identical to the whole sector
Nominal GVA share (FY 2025–26 PE)~25.8%
3. Tertiary Sector (Service Sector)
FunctionProvides services to both Primary and Secondary sectors
ActivitiesBanking, Trade, Insurance, Transport, Storage, Education, Health, Tourism, Public Administration
WorkersWhite collar: informal office/professional label, not the whole services sector
Nominal GVA share (FY 2025–26 PE)~54.3% (Highest)
4. Quaternary Sector
  • Knowledge-based services: Culture, Library, Research, Education, Information Technology
  • Also called the "Knowledge Sector"
5. Quinary Sector
  • Related to the highest level of decision-makers or policy-makers
  • Highest-level strategic leadership and policy decisions
  • Extended conceptual category, not a separate official Indian national-accounts broad sector
  • Top leadership of government, businesses, universities, research institutions and major organisations
