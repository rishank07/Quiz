Foreign Trade — Overview

Foreign Trade: Exchange of goods and services between two or more countries.

2.4%Share in world merchandise + commercial-services exports (2022)
3.0%Share in world merchandise + commercial-services imports (2022)
Balance of Trade (BOT)
  • Difference between monetary value of a country's exports and imports of goods during a specific period
  • Only related to tangible goods (goods that can be touched or seen)
  • Traditional means of entering foreign exchange: Exports
TermCondition
Trade SurplusExports > Imports
Trade DeficitExports < Imports
⚠ EXAM TRAP
BOT covers ONLY tangible goods, while BOP's Current Account covers both goods AND services — don't treat BOT and Current Account as identical.

Four Pillars

  • Promotion of exports through alliances with exporters, states, districts, and Indian missions
  • Incentive to Remission
  • Facilitating e-commerce in emerging sectors, retail infrastructure development, and SCOMET policy
  • Improving ease of doing business, reducing cost of business, and promoting e-portals
Related Trade Concepts
  • Import Substitution Policy: An "Inward-looking Trade Strategy"
  • Dumping: Exporting below normal value, generally the comparable domestic-market price; prescribed below-cost cases can also be relevant
  • Quota: Determination of import and export limits
Balance of Payments (BOP)

A systematic record of all economic transactions between residents of a country and the rest of the world during a given period.

ComponentDetails
Current AccountGoods, services, primary income, secondary income/current transfers (including remittances); closely linked with income, production, demand and employment
Capital AccountConventional Indian capital/financial account: FDI, portfolio flows, external borrowings, banking capital, NRI deposits and other external assets/liabilities; these flows can affect the real economy
Current Account DeficitTotal current-account debits exceed credits; broader than merchandise trade deficit
Twin DeficitsCurrent Account Deficit + Fiscal Deficit
⚠ EXAM TRAP
Current account includes goods, services, primary and secondary income. Capital/financial flows can also affect output and employment through investment, credit, exchange rates and financial conditions.
Foreign Exchange Rate

Foreign exchange rate = price of one currency in terms of another; a hypothetical example is US$1 = ₹87.

Fixed Exchange Rate System

  • Controlled by government intervention using government reserves

Floating rate: primarily market-determined; authorities can still intervene in a managed float. Convertibility and floating are different concepts.

Exchange Rate System in India

  • Managed exchange rate system since 1993
  • Based on market demand and supply
  • RBI intervenes when necessary
Currency Devaluation

Deliberately reducing the value of one's own currency in terms of foreign currencies.

  • Imports become costlier in domestic currency; actual import volumes depend on demand elasticities and other factors
  • Exports become cheaper to foreign buyers; actual export volumes depend on demand elasticities and other factors
Foreign Exchange Reserve
  • Foreign assets
  • Gold reserves
  • Reserve Tranche Position (RTP) in IMF
  • Special Drawing Rights (SDR)

Foreign Direct Investment (FDI)

Cross-border investment intended to establish lasting interest and significant influence/control; generally 10% or more equity ownership under India's classification

Greenfield InvestmentDevelopment of a new unit/infrastructure
Brownfield InvestmentInvestment in an already existing unit

FDI Restrictions (Sectors Where FDI Not Allowed)

  • Lottery business
  • Betting
  • Chit funds
  • Real-estate business/construction of farm houses, with policy-defined exceptions
  • Railway Operations
  • Nidhi Company
  • Gambling
  • Manufacturing specified tobacco products; atomic energy; trading in TDRs
⚠ EXAM TRAP
FDI is restricted in "Railway Operations" but Railway-RELATED PROJECTS (infrastructure like tracks, stations) allow 100% FDI — a subtle but important distinction.
Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI)
  • Purchase of less than 10% shares of a company by a foreign investor
  • Generally liquid and potentially volatile/reversible compared with FDI
  • More liquid and potentially more volatile/reversible than FDI; not inherently unsafe
Foreign Institutional Investment (FII)

Older FII, Sub-Account and QFI categories merged into the unified FPI regime from June 2014

⚡ QUICK RECALL
FDI generally involves lasting interest/influence and 10% or more equity; FPI is below 10% under the relevant framework and is generally more liquid/reversible. FII is an older category merged into FPI from June 2014.
