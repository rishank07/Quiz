Poverty and Unemployment

Poverty Measurement · Committees · MPI · Unemployment Types · Govt. Schemes — SSC / Railway / UPSC / BPSC

Poverty — Meaning
  • Lack of access to basic necessities (food, shelter, clothing)
  • According to Amartya Sen, poverty is a multi-dimensional problem
Ways to Measure Poverty
TypeDescription
Absolute PovertyPoverty against a fixed minimum living standard/threshold. Boyd Orr linked poverty, poor diet and ill health and became FAO's first Director-General; he is not credited here with first using absolute poverty.
Relative PovertyLack of income/consumption compared to average living standard; measured using Lorenz Curve (Max O. Lorenz, 1905 — income inequality) and Gini Coefficient (Corrado Gini, 1912 — income/wealth inequality)
⚠ EXAM TRAP
Lorenz Curve (1905) and Gini Coefficient (1912) are related but different tools — Lorenz is a graphical curve, Gini is a numerical coefficient derived from it.
Poverty Line & Recall Methods

Poverty Line: Level of income or consumption expenditure below which a person is considered poor. Poverty is based on consumption expenditure.

MethodDescription
Uniform Recall PeriodFood and non-food items considered for the last 30 days
Mixed Recall Period365-day recall: clothing/bedding, footwear, education, institutional medical care and durables; 30 days for other items
Head Count Ratio
  • Measures absolute poverty
  • Percentage of population below the poverty line
  • Sen Poverty Index combines headcount ratio, average poverty gap and inequality among the poor; it does not simply replace HCR with an income-gap ratio
Poverty Assessment in India
Name / CommitteeYearKey Facts
Dadabhai Naoroji1867–68Pioneering pre-Independence estimate using the cost of a subsistence diet; Poverty and Un-British Rule in India was published in 1901
VM Dandekar & N. Rath1971Widely cited systematic post-Independence poverty assessment using NSS data; approximately 2,250 calories/person/day for both rural and urban areas
Y.K. Alagh Committee19792400 calories in rural areas; 2100 calories in urban areas
Lakdawala Committee1993Separate CPI for rural and urban areas; CPI-IW for industrial workers; CPI-AL for agricultural labourers
Suresh Tendulkar CommitteeConstituted December 2005; report December 2009Moved away from direct calorie norms, used a common urban poverty-line reference basket, and included private health/education spending. 2011–12 MPCE: Rural ₹816/month (25.7% poor); Urban ₹1,000 (13.7%); All India 21.9%.
C. Rangarajan CommitteeFormed 2012; Report 20142011–12 poverty lines: rural ₹972/person/month; urban ₹1,407; all-India poverty ratio ~29.5%

Consumption measurement now uses NSO HCES with Modified Mixed Reference Period (MMRP); the last official Tendulkar poverty ratio remains the 2011–12 estimate.

⚠ EXAM TRAP
Dandekar–Rath (1971): about 2,250 calories in rural and urban areas. Alagh Task Force (1979): rural 2,400; urban 2,100 calories. Tendulkar group: constituted 2005, reported 2009.

Unemployment — Meaning & Types

Unemployment: without work while seeking/available for work under survey rules. Causes include insufficient suitable jobs, skills mismatch, structural change, seasonality and underemployment; not overpopulation alone.

TypeDescription
Seasonal UnemploymentWork is available only in particular seasons or parts of the year; duration varies by activity
Frictional UnemploymentTime gap between leaving one job and joining another
Disguised UnemploymentMore workers than needed; some can withdraw without reducing output (zero marginal productivity is the textbook limiting case)
Cyclical UnemploymentUnemployment caused by downturns in aggregate demand and business-cycle contractions
Open UnemploymentAble and willing to work, seeking/available for work, but no employment
Structural UnemploymentMismatch between workers' skills and job requirements
Long-term UnemploymentSituation of unemployment for a long duration
⚠ EXAM TRAP
Disguised Unemployment (common in Indian agriculture) vs Cyclical Unemployment (common in developed/industrial economies) — a frequently confused pair.
Bhagwati Committee (appointed 1970; final report 1973) — Activity-Status Measures
ConceptDescription
Usual StatusActivity status over a 365-day reference period; captures relatively long-duration/chronic employment or unemployment
Current Weekly StatusNo work even for one hour in the preceding seven days, while seeking or available for work
Current Daily StatusEmployment/unemployment in person-days, including half-days, for each day of the reference week; sensitive to underemployment
Key Terms & Formula
Full EmploymentCyclical unemployment essentially absent; employment near potential, not literally zero unemployment
Labour ForceNo. of employed + No. of unemployed
Headline PLFS reporting age (India)15 years and above (headline PLFS indicators)
Working Age Population (OECD)15–64 years
Unemployment Rate = (No. of Unemployed ÷ Labour Force) × 100
⚡ QUICK RECALL
India's headline PLFS indicators commonly cover ages 15 and above; OECD's conventional working-age range is 15–64. These are different reporting conventions.
