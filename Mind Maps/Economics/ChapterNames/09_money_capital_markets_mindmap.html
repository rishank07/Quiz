Financial Market — Overview
  • Buyers and sellers purchase and sell securities, debentures, and bonds
  • Two components: Money Market and Capital Market
Money Market

Deals in short-term financial instruments (maturity up to 1 year).

Call Money Market

TypeMaturity
Call Money1 day
Notice Money2 to 14 days
Term Money15 to 365 days
⚠ EXAM TRAP
Call Money (1 day) vs Notice Money (2–14 days) vs Term Money (15–365 days) — a classic ascending-maturity sequence tested in order-matching questions.
Tools of the Money Market
InstrumentKey Facts
Commercial BillShort-term trade bill arising from credit sales; common tenors 30/60/90 days. Businesses draw bills; banks/financial institutions discount or rediscount them
Commercial PaperIntroduced 1990; unsecured promissory-note instrument; minimum ₹5 lakh and multiples; maturity 7 days to 1 year; eligible companies, NBFCs/AIFIs and other permitted resident entities under RBI conditions
Certificate of Deposit (CD)First in New York (1960s); Initiated by RBI in 1989; Minimum denomination ₹5 lakh and multiples of ₹5 lakh; Maturity: 7 days to 1 year; Issued by Commercial Banks, RRBs, Small Finance Banks
Treasury Bill (TB)Zero-coupon Central Government security issued at a discount; RBI auctions for Government of India. Minimum ₹10,000 face value and multiples; active tenors 91/182/364 days. Marketable 14-day bill introduced in 1997
⚡ QUICK RECALL
Treasury Bills are Central Government securities; RBI auctions them on the government's behalf. They are zero-coupon instruments issued at a discount to face value.
Capital Market — Overview
  • Deals in long-term instruments (maturity greater than 1 year)
  • Long-term transactions via shares, securities, bonds, debentures, and mutual funds
  • Two sub-markets: Primary Market and Secondary Market
Primary Market
  • Involves the first-time sale of new securities
  • Market related to new issuances
  • Raises both domestic and foreign funds
Domestic InstrumentsEquity shares by companies; debt instruments by govt./financial intermediaries
Foreign InstrumentsGDR, ADR, Foreign Commercial Borrowing
IPOInitial Public Offer — shares issued for the first time by newly listed companies
FPOFollow-on Public Offer — additional shares issued by already-listed companies to raise extra capital
⚠ EXAM TRAP
IPO = first-time share issue by a company going public; FPO = additional share issue by an ALREADY listed company — commonly confused.
Secondary Market
  • Trading of existing/previously issued securities
  • Registered stock exchange operating under law
  • No direct fresh capital to issuer in ordinary secondary trades; liquidity indirectly supports primary-market capital formation
  • Includes: Mutual Funds, Financial Institutions, Share Brokers, etc.
Gilt-edged Market & Stock Market
Gilt-edged MarketGovernment-securities market: very low default risk but still subject to interest-rate and market-price risk
Stock MarketTerm used for the secondary market; trading of shares, debt instruments, bonds, govt. securities

National Stock Exchange (NSE)
Established1992
PurposeNationwide, professionally managed, screen-based securities market
HeadquartersBandra Kurla Complex, Mumbai
Original PromotersLeading Indian financial institutions, banks, insurers and financial intermediaries

India's largest and most technologically advanced stock exchange; also the first fully computerised stock exchange.

MIBOR and MIBID

  • Historically paired NSE MIBID/MIBOR reference rates; current overnight MIBOR is published by FBIL
  • MIBOR: Mumbai Interbank Outright Rate (current FBIL overnight rupee benchmark)
  • MIBID: Mumbai Inter Bank Bid Rate
  • LIBOR: London Interbank Offered Rate; most major panel-bank settings discontinued by June 2023

Nifty 50: NSE benchmark diversified 50-stock equity index.

Securities and Exchange Board of India (SEBI)
Statutory BasisSEBI Act, 1992
Established12 April, 1988
Statutory Status Granted30 January 1992, under the SEBI Act framework
HeadquartersMumbai
Regional OfficesChennai, Delhi, Kolkata, Ahmedabad
  • Controls and regulates transactions in the stock market
  • Grants recognition to stock exchanges
  • August 2008: NSE launched exchange-traded currency futures in India
  • Merger of Forward Markets Commission (FMC) with SEBI, September 2015
⚠ EXAM TRAP
SEBI was established in 1988 but got statutory power only in 1992 — a 4-year gap that's a common date-based trap.

Insider trading: securities trading while possessing material non-public information, or otherwise violating insider-trading rules; not restricted to employees.
