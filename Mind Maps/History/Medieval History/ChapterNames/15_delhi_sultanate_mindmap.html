💰 Economic

  • Waqf = land for religious purposes
  • Abolished Khut, Muqaddam, Chaudhary
  • Booty (Khums): 1/5 to soldiers, 4/5 to treasury
  • Land measurement: Masahat → Officer: Diwan-e-Mustakhraj

  • Standing army system
  • Cash salary to soldiers
  • Branding horses: Dagh system

  • Food items market: Mandi
  • Cloth market: Sarai Adl
  • Market for horses, sheep, goats, slaves
📋 Taxes on Non-Muslims
  • Jizya — religious tax
  • Kharaj — land tax
  • Ghari tax — house tax
  • Charai tax — pasture tax

⚠️ Exam Traps — Delhi Sultanate
Trap #1 — Qutub Minar — Started vs Completed

Qutub-ud-Din Aibak started construction of the Qutub Minar. Iltutmish completed the 73-metre structure. Therefore, Aibak started it, while Iltutmish completed it.

Trap #2 — Aibak's Death

Qutub-ud-Din Aibak died while playing Chaugan (polo), not in battle. He died at Lahore in 1210 CE.

Trap #3 — Turkan-i-Chahalgani

Iltutmish formed the group of forty nobles known as Turkan-i-Chahalgani. Balban later abolished it. These were two different actions.

Trap #4 — Razia Sultana's Death

Razia was assassinated by members of the Khokhar tribe at Kaithal, Haryana, on 13 October 1240. She was killed at Kaithal, not Delhi.

Trap #5 — Longest Dynasty

The Tughlaq dynasty (1320–1412 CE) ruled the Delhi Sultanate for the longest period, not the Slave or Lodi dynasty.

Trap #6 — Two Books Named "Tarikh-i-Firozshahi"

Two different works share the title Tarikh-i-Firozshahi: Ziauddin Barani wrote one during Muhammad bin Tughlaq’s period, and Shams-i-Siraj Afif wrote the other during Firoz Shah Tughlaq’s period. Match both the author and the ruler carefully.

Trap #7 — Kohinoor Diamond

Malik Kafur obtained the Kohinoor diamond from the ruler Prataparudra Deva during the Warangal campaign of 1309 CE, not during the Gujarat or Chittor campaign.

Trap #8 — Token Currency Failed

Muhammad bin Tughlaq introduced copper and brass token currency, but the reform failed because of widespread counterfeiting and a lack of public trust. It was not a successful reform.

Trap #9 — Akbar of Sultanate Period

Elliot and Elphinstone called Firoz Shah Tughlaq the “Akbar of the Sultanate Period”, not Muhammad bin Tughlaq. Firoz is remembered as a welfare-oriented ruler.

Trap #10 — Agra founded by?

Sikandar Lodi founded Agra in 1504 CE, not Babur or Akbar. It became his capital in 1506 CE.
