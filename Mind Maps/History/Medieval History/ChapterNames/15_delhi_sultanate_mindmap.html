Qutub-ud-Din Aibak1206–1210 CE
  • Titles: Lakh Bakhsh, Malik, Sipahsalar
  • Slave of Muhammad Ghori
  • Capital: Lahore (initially)
  • Death: while playing Chaugan (Polo) at Lahore, 1210 CE
  • Court scholars: Hasan Nizami and Fakhr-i-Mudabbir
  • Built Quwwat-ul-Islam Mosque (Mehrauli, Delhi)
  • Started construction of Qutub Minar
  • Built Adhai Din Ka Jhopra (Ajmer)
Iltutmish1211–1236 CE
  • Governor of Badaun; son-in-law of Qutub-ud-Din Aibak
  • Organized the iqta system of revenue assignments
  • Wazir: Nizam-ul-Mulk Junaidi
  • Formed group of 40 nobles → Turkan-i-Chahalgani
  • Issued: Silver coins = Tanka | Copper coins = Jital
  • Important coin inscriptions were in Arabic; this describes language, not metal purity
  • Capital shifted to Delhi
  • Title: Sultan-e-Azam
  • Added three storeys to Qutub Minar; its present height is about 73 metres
  • Suppressed Bengal rebellion in 1225 CE
  • Immediate successor: Ruknuddin Firuz; Razia succeeded later in 1236
  • Built Sultangarhi Tomb — in memory of son Nasiruddin
  • Iltutmish Tomb built in 1235 CE
Razia Sultana1236–1240 CE
  • First Muslim woman ruler of medieval India
  • Daughter of Iltutmish
  • Rejected purdah, adopted Kuba (coat) and Kulah (turban)
  • Wazir Nizam-ul-Mulk Junaidi opposed her
  • Close associate: Yaqut Khan (Abyssinian)
  • Rebellion by Governor of Bhatinda — Altuniya
  • Yaqut Khan killed; Razia imprisoned → later married Altuniya
  • Patronized Minhaj-i-Siraj; his later Tabaqat-i-Nasiri was dedicated to Nasiruddin Mahmud
  • Killed near Kaithal (Haryana), October 1240; the killers’ precise identity is uncertain
Balban1266–1287 CE
  • Previously served as Naib (Deputy) of Sultan Nasiruddin Mahmud
  • Titles: Ulugh Khan, Zil-e-Ilahi (Shadow of God)
  • Caste: Ilbari Turk
  • Claimed descent from the legendary Turanian king Afrasiyab
  • Introduced Sijda (prostration) and Paibos (kissing feet)
  • Celebrated the Persian New Year festival Nauroz at court
  • Reduced the political influence of Turkan-e-Chahalgani (Group of 40)
  • Spy system — spies called Barid
  • Established Diwan-e-Arz (military department)
  • Policy of Blood and Iron
  • Emphasized strict justice even against powerful nobles
Qaiqabad and Kayumars → End of Slave Dynasty1290 CE
  • After Qaiqabad’s reign and Kayumars’ brief rule, Jalaluddin took power in 1290 CE
  • End of Slave Dynasty → Beginning of Khilji Dynasty

Key Reforms
💰 Economic
  • Milk = privately held property or land
  • Waqf = land for religious purposes
  • Curtailed privileges and revenue exemptions of Khut, Muqaddam, Chaudhary
  • Booty (Khums): 1/5 to soldiers, 4/5 to treasury
  • Masahat = land measurement; Diwan-e-Mustakhraj = recovery of revenue arrears
⚔️ Military
  • Standing army system
  • Cash salary to soldiers
  • Branding horses: Dagh system
🏪 3 Markets
  • Food items market: Mandi
  • Cloth market: Sarai Adl
  • Market for horses, sheep, goats, slaves
📋 Taxes and Revenue
  • Jizya — poll tax on liable non-Muslim subjects
  • Kharaj — land tax
  • Ghari tax — house tax
  • Charai tax — pasture tax

Ghiyasuddin Tughlaq1320–1325 CE

  • Campaign of 1324 CE — intervened in Bengal; defeated Harisimhadeva in Tirhut

⚠️ Exam Traps — Delhi Sultanate
Trap #1 — Qutub Minar — Started vs Completed

Aibak began Qutub Minar; Iltutmish added three storeys. Firoz Shah Tughlaq later rebuilt its damaged upper part. The present tower is about 73 metres high.

Trap #2 — Aibak's Death

Qutub-ud-Din Aibak died while playing Chaugan (polo), not in battle. He died at Lahore in 1210 CE.

Trap #3 — Turkan-i-Chahalgani

Iltutmish formed Turkan-i-Chahalgani, the group of forty nobles. Balban later reduced their political power to strengthen royal authority.

Trap #4 — Razia Sultana's Death

Razia and Altuniya were killed near Kaithal, Haryana, in October 1240, after failing to regain Delhi. The killers’ precise identity is uncertain.

Trap #5 — Longest Dynasty

The Tughlaq dynasty (1320–1414 CE) ruled the Delhi Sultanate for the longest period, not the Slave or Lodi dynasty.

Trap #6 — Two Books Named "Tarikh-i-Firozshahi"

Barani’s Tarikh-i-Firozshahi covers reigns from Balban to early Firoz Shah. Afif’s work of the same title chiefly concerns Firoz Shah’s reign; they are different histories.

Trap #7 — Kohinoor Diamond

Malik Kafur secured Prataparudra II’s submission at Warangal in 1310, during the 1309–10 campaign. Identifying a tribute diamond specifically as the Kohinoor is uncertain.

Trap #8 — Token Currency Failed

Muhammad bin Tughlaq introduced copper and brass token currency, but the reform failed because of widespread counterfeiting and a lack of public trust. It was not a successful reform.

Trap #9 — Firoz Shah’s Public Works

Firoz Shah Tughlaq sponsored canals, towns and charitable departments. A comparison with Akbar is a later evaluation, not an official title, and modern PWD terminology is anachronistic.

Trap #10 — Agra founded by?

Sikandar Lodi founded Agra in 1504 CE and developed it as an important seat of government.
